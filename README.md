*Note: The cross-sectional of the wire that the current passes through is not a constant but depends on frequency
When an alternating current flows through a conductor, it produces a changing magnetic field inside the conductor. According to Faraday–Lenz's law, the changing magnetic field induces eddy currents. These eddy currents flow in such a direction that the magnetic field they produce opposes the change in the original magnetic field. As a result, in the central region of the conductor, the eddy currents oppose the original AC current, making the current weaker. Near the surface, the eddy currents reinforce the original AC current, making the current stronger. Therefore, the current is no longer uniformly distributed across the conductor's cross-section as it is with DC current, but tends to concentrate near the surface of the conductor.

Skin depth (δ) is the effective depth near the surface of a conductor through which most of the AC current flows. As the frequency increases, the skin depth decreases, reducing the effective cross-sectional area and increasing the AC resistance. ( Because resistance of the materials is inversely proportional to the effective cross-sectional area).

# Proximity effect:
* When two conductors are placed close to each other and carry alternating currents, the current in each conductor produces a changing magnetic field. The magnetic field from one conductor affects the nearby conductor, inducing eddy currents in it. According to Lenz's law, these eddy currents produce a magnetic field that opposes the change in the magnetic field that caused them. As a result, the current in each conductor is no longer uniformly distributed across its cross-section but becomes concentrated toward one side.
* When two conductors carry currents in the same direction, the current tends to concentrate toward the outer sides, away from each other. In contrast, when the conductors carry currents in opposite directions, the current tends to concentrate toward the inner sides, closer to each other.Finally, the decrease in the effective cross-sectional area leads to an increase in resistance and power loss.
